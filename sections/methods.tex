\section{Methods}\label{methods}

\subsection{LongReadAlignmentAssembler (LRAA) algorithm}\label{longreadalignmentassembler-lraa-algorithm}

We developed LongReadAlignmentAssembler (LRAA) as a method for transcript isoform discovery and quantification from long-read RNA sequencing data. LRAA reconstructs full-length transcript isoforms by constructing a splice graph from long RNA-seq read alignment-supported exons and introns, enumerating read-consistent paths through this graph, and selecting high-confidence isoforms via iterative best-path extraction. Isoform abundances are estimated using an expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm that resolves ambiguous read assignments.

LRAA has three primary execution modes: (1) annotation-free isoform discovery and quantification, requiring only aligned reads and a reference genome; (2) reference annotation-guided isoform discovery and quantification, where known gene models inform splice-graph construction and filtering; and (3) quantification-only mode, where isoform discovery is bypassed and reads are directly assigned to reference isoforms for abundance estimation via EM.

LRAA supports PacBio HiFi and ONT data through two platform-adapted execution modes. HiFi mode (-\/-HiFi) requires higher alignment identity (97\% versus 80\%) and, relying on the accuracy of HiFi base calls, retains less abundant alternative splice junctions and isoforms (minimum fractions of 0.5\% versus 3\% for junctions and 0.5\% versus 1\% for isoforms). It also keeps alternative splice boundaries that lie within 5 nt of a stronger junction, which default mode merges into that junction when they carry at most 20\% of its support, and it tolerates only shorter indels in transcriptome rescue alignments (under 10 bp versus under 30 bp). Both modes infer transcription start sites (TSS) and polyadenylation (PolyA) sites from read termini. Unless otherwise noted, the parameter values and algorithmic decisions described below correspond to HiFi mode, and most thresholds are user-configurable.

\subsection{Input and preprocessing}\label{input-and-preprocessing}

LRAA requires coordinate-sorted BAM files of long RNA-seq reads aligned to a reference genome (typically via minimap2), along with the reference genome in FASTA format. Reference gene annotations in GTF format are required for quantification-only mode or reference annotation-guided isoform discovery. Annotation-free isoform identification and quantification requires only the input alignment BAM and reference genome.

\subsubsection{Filtering of alignments}\label{filtering-of-alignments}

LRAA uses only primary alignments. Alignments are further discarded according to the following criteria: supplementary alignment status, percent identity is below the minimum threshold (97\% in HiFi mode, 80\% otherwise), contains an intron longer than 200 kb (default), or overlaps a genomic region matching the human rDNA repeat unit by at least 50 bp. Reads removed only by the identity filter remain candidates for transcriptome-based alignment rescue (see below).

\subsubsection{Normalization of read coverage for isoform discovery}\label{normalization-of-read-coverage-for-isoform-discovery}

To limit computation in very deep regions and enable scalability to exceedingly large data sets, alignments are thinned separately for each strand toward a depth of 1,000. Each alignment is kept with probability $\min(1, 1000/d)$, where $d$ is the median depth of the 100-bp windows its aligned blocks cover. Each alignment kept in a region deeper than 1,000 is weighted by the inverse of that probability, $d/1000$, and stands in for that many reads. Alignments carrying a junction supported by fewer than 1,000 alignments are always kept, so the support of such junctions is exact (weight 1). In all three modes the thinned, weighted alignments are used to build the splice graph and to estimate isoform proportions, and every alignment of the filtered BAM is then assigned to isoforms at those proportions (see Isoform quantification).

\subsubsection{Alignment correction and soft-clipped termini adjustments}\label{alignment-correction-and-soft-clipped-termini-adjustments}

Short read segments next to a splice junction often fail to align across it, so the aligner soft-clips them or extends the alignment a few bases into the intron instead of aligning them to the neighboring exon. LRAA tests whether such read ends can be realigned to a neighboring exon, using the splice graph to find the candidates: the exons connected by a splice-graph intron that ends at the exon boundary where the read is soft-clipped. Specifically, for a read with a 5--20 base soft clip whose terminal aligned block reaches that boundary or extends a few bases past it into the intron, LRAA compares the clipped bases, together with any bases aligned into the intron (at most 20 in total), with the genomic sequence at the end of each candidate intron-connected exon, guided by the splice graph. At the first exact match, the bases are realigned to that exon and the connecting intron is added to the read's alignment. Separately, a 3\p{} soft clip of at least 7 bases that is at least 80\% adenine (thymine on the reverse strand), over the whole clip or its 20 bases nearest the alignment, is treated as a post-transcriptional poly(A) tail. LRAA removes the clip, and the read's aligned 3\p{} end becomes a candidate polyadenylation site.

\subsection{Splice-graph construction}\label{splice-graph-construction}

\textbf{Initial splice graph construction}: For each genomic contig and strand, LRAA constructs a directed acyclic graph representing the exon--intron architecture inferred from read alignments. Graph nodes correspond to contiguous genomic intervals supported by read coverage (exon segments) or splice junctions (introns). Introns supported by spliced read alignments become nodes that connect the corresponding exon nodes in the graph. Read-derived introns are admitted only when their terminal dinucleotides form a canonical GT-AG, GC-AG or AT-AC splice motif on the corresponding strand of the graph. Coordinate-contiguous exon segments are merged along unbranched exon-only graph paths. When input transcript models are provided, reference exonic regions and introns are incorporated and LRAA fractures any preexisting contiguous coverage segments in the splice graph at their transcript boundaries. Non-canonical splice motifs are allowed for reference annotation-supported splice patterns.

Read support is quantified differently for exon and intron nodes. For introns, each spliced-read alignment containing the corresponding donor--acceptor junction adds its normalization weight to the intron's support. For exon segments, weighted genomic base coverage is accumulated across aligned reads, and the mean coverage across each exon segment is assigned as its support value. These support values are used to filter and prune splice-graph features and therefore affect which paths are available for isoform reconstruction, as described below.

\textbf{TSS and PolyA site node integration}: LRAA infers TSS and PolyA sites from read termini with minimal allowed soft clipping (default, 0 bases). Read termini positions within 50 bp are aggregated, and clusters with weighted support of at least five alignments are retained as initial candidates. TSS candidates must also pass a fivefold local-enrichment test, \(\frac{C_p+1}{\widetilde{C}_{p,\pm 50}+1}\geq 5\), where \(C_p\) is the weighted alignment count at the peak position and \(\widetilde{C}_{p,\pm 50}\) is the median of the positive raw counts at all other positions from \(p-50\) through \(p+49\), augmented with two zero-valued observations. This two zero padding provides a low background estimate in sparse windows and limits the influence of one or two neighboring endpoints on an otherwise isolated peak. LRAA then incorporates TSS and PolyA sites as splice-graph nodes and filters them within each connected component according to their fractions of total TSS or PolyA support, as described below. In the splice graph of spliced isoforms, read-derived PolyA candidates whose downstream genomic sequence meets the internal-priming criteria (see Internal priming annotation and filter) are discarded unless a user-supplied set of known cleavage sites (-\/-polyA\_known) has a site within 25 nt. Isoform ends that do not reach a TSS or PolyA site are placed after reconstruction (see Isoform terminal exon boundary refinement).

\textbf{Pruning of lowly supported introns}: To remove spurious features arising from alignment errors, LRAA prunes the splice graph using several criteria. Non-reference introns longer than 200 kb are removed. Introns supported by fewer than two reads are also removed unless they match known junctions in a provided reference annotation. Competing introns that share a donor or acceptor boundary are evaluated separately for each boundary. The most highly supported intron in each group is retained, while non-reference alternatives are removed if they account for less than 0.5\% of the group's junction support in HiFi mode or 3\% in default ONT mode. In default ONT mode, LRAA performs adjacent splice boundary aggregation by removing non-reference alternatives whose opposite splice boundary lies within 5 nt of the representative junction and that carry at most 20\% of its support; this adjacent-boundary aggregation is disabled in HiFi mode. A later exon-island pass compares introns attached along each contiguous exon chain, including alternatives without a shared donor or acceptor, and removes introns whose support is below the minimum alternative-splice fraction of the most highly supported intron in that island. Introns disconnected from an upstream or downstream exon by exon pruning are also removed.

\textbf{Pruning of lowly supported unspliced exon segments}: Exon segments that overlap introns are evaluated as potential low-support unspliced sequence. For exon segments spanned by introns, a segment is removed when its mean coverage divided by the overlapping intron's alignment count is below the minimum alternative-unspliced fraction (default, 1\%). This test is restricted to introns no longer than 10 kb and supported by more than 100 alignments. Exon segments adjacent to a detected splice boundary or overlapping an exon from an input transcript annotation are retained. Intron-overlapping exon segment pruning is not applied in quantification-only mode.

\textbf{Pruning of lowly supported TSS and PolyA sites}: After this initial feature pruning and TSS/PolyA integration, the graph is initially partitioned into weakly connected components, each ideally representing a candidate gene locus. When TSS and PolyA nodes are present, LRAA performs support-based filtering within each preliminary component. TSS sites representing less than the minimum TSS isoform fraction of total TSS-supporting reads in the component are removed as likely noise (default, 5\%). PolyA sites are filtered analogously, using a minimum PolyA fraction (default, 1\%). TSS sites are further examined for potential RNA degradation artifacts: the graph is traversed along linear exon paths from each high-support TSS, and transcriptionally downstream alternative TSS sites with ≤20\% of the support of the TSS where the walk started are removed as probable 5\p{} degradation products; when the `TSS cap signature rules' below are on, a capped alternative TSS is exempt, and uncapped TSS sites downstream of a capped site are removed before the fraction filter.

\textbf{TSS sites and the 5\p{} cap signature}: In template-switching cDNA protocols, reverse transcriptase adds an untemplated C opposite the 5\p{} cap, so reads that start at a capped RNA end carry one more G at their 5\p{} end than the library's other reads \citep{wulf2019, cumbie2015}. LRAA counts the run of G's at the end of each read's 5\p{} soft clip, next to the first aligned base. It uses reads with no clip, a clip of one to three G's, or a longer clip ending in at least three G's, such as an untrimmed template-switching oligo, and skips reads whose start is preceded by a genomic G. For these reads, a G at the 5\p{} end could be the transcript's own templated G or the untemplated cap G, and the alignment cannot tell the two apart: whether that G was clipped or aligned, the count cannot be trusted. A read is capped when its count exceeds the library's baseline, the most common count among spliced read starts away from TSS peaks (0 for the single-cell Kinnex libraries, 1 for bulk PacBio Kinnex and 3 for ONT cDNA from SG-NEx, whose untrimmed reads still carry the template-switching oligo and its three terminal G's \citep{an2025}). Before discovery, LRAA computes the capped fraction of reads at each TSS peak. In a library with the signature, peaks fall into a capped group (promoters) and an uncapped group (truncation and degradation pile-ups). LRAA separates the two at Otsu's threshold \citep{otsu1979} and turns the cap rules on when the capped group is large and distinct (at least 50 peaks and 10\% of peaks, with a median capped fraction at least 0.20 above the uncapped group's). Libraries without the signature, such as spike-ins, simulated reads or libraries with trimmed 5\p{} ends, leave the rules off, and their TSS sites rest on read support alone. With the rules on, a TSS site downstream of a capped site is pruned when fewer than 10\% of its reads are capped. In the cluster-guided phase of the single-cell workflow, TSS sites found only in a cluster's reads, and not in the guiding annotation, must also show evidence of capping (at least two capped reads), so that 5\p{} truncation pile-ups in a cluster's sparse reads do not introduce false starts.

\textbf{Pruning of exon spurs}: An exon spur is a short exonic segment ($\leq$13 bp) that extends past a splice site into the intron and ends there, with no TSS or PolyA site attached, while the exon is also spliced at that site; such spurs reflect common alignment artifacts. After boundary filtering, LRAA considers such segments for pruning from the splice graph. Segments overlapping exons from an input transcript annotation are retained. LRAA then finalizes the graph and recomputes its weakly connected components to account for topology changes caused by boundary and terminal-spur pruning.

\subsection{Read compatibility class (RCC) definitions and RCC graph construction}\label{read-compatibility-class-rcc-definitions-and-rcc-graph-construction}

Each read alignment is represented as a labeled splice-graph read path, defined as an ordered sequence of splice-graph nodes encoding the exon--intron structure traversed by the read. Reads with identical splice structure are aggregated as \textbf{Read Compatibility Classes} (\textbf{RCCs}), and their labeled read paths are compiled into a directed acyclic graph (the \textbf{RCC Graph}). Each node in the RCC Graph represents a unique consecutive sequence of splice-graph nodes and is annotated with genomic coordinates, read support count, and boundary flags indicating whether the path begins or ends at an inferred TSS or PolyA site.

RCCs differ from the transcript compatibility counts (TCCs) used by Isosceles and short-read pseudoaligners \citep{ntranos2016, kabza2024, bray2016}. A TCC groups reads by the set of transcripts they are compatible with, and so requires the transcript set to be defined first; reads with different structures that are compatible with the same transcripts fall into a single TCC. An RCC instead groups reads by their own splice-graph path, including any TSS and PolyA boundary nodes it reaches, before any isoform is defined. RCCs therefore serve both as the units from which isoforms are assembled and as the units of quantification, and because each RCC retains its path, the EM can weight its assignment to each compatible isoform individually, by agreement between the read and isoform 3\p{} ends (see Isoform quantification).

Before RCC relationships are evaluated, each RCC is assigned to the weakly connected component of the underlying splice graph containing the first node of its path. To limit computation in complex loci, if one of these preliminary groups contains more than 1,000 RCCs, LRAA retains the 1,000 RCCs with the greatest read support, using path sequence length to break support ties. LRAA then compares each pair of overlapping RCCs among those retained. Two RCC paths are compatible when their splice structures agree wherever they overlap. Compatible pairs fall into two cases: when one path lies entirely within the other, the contained RCC is attached to its containing RCC as supporting evidence; when each path extends beyond the other, an RCC graph edge can link them so that reconstruction may chain them into a longer path. In the default collapse-only configuration, LRAA draws no such edges, so each reconstructed isoform follows the path of a single RCC together with the RCCs it contains, rather than a chain of overlapping read paths.

At the start of isoform reconstruction, LRAA partitions the retained RCCs into disjoint components by transitively grouping RCCs that share splice-graph nodes. Each resulting component is processed independently for isoform reconstruction.

\subsection{Isoform identification}\label{isoform-identification}

LRAA reconstructs transcript isoforms from the RCC graph using a trellis-based candidate-path procedure followed by iterative score-based selection. It creates one trellis vertex for each eligible RCC node, ordered by genomic coordinates and terminal-boundary status. RCCs contained by more than ten other RCCs are omitted as vertices, although their reads remain represented through the containing RCCs. Each vertex initially retains a singleton candidate consisting of its own RCC. The trellis implements a PASA-inspired path-extension mechanism \citep{haas2003}: in configurations that permit RCC chaining, LRAA evaluates extensions from each predecessor and retains the highest-scoring compatible extension from that predecessor. In the default PacBio and ONT collapse-only modes, however, edges between compatible but non-contained RCCs are suppressed, and reconstruction is limited to containment collapsing. Structural candidates are therefore singleton RCC nodes, with reads from contained RCCs contributing to the evidence represented by their containing candidates.

Each candidate is scored by the number of distinct reads represented by its directly included RCC nodes and the RCCs recursively recorded as their containments. A read contributes at most once to a candidate's score, even if multiple nodes represent it. TSS and PolyA annotations influence compatibility and containment but do not contribute to path scoring. In reference-guided mode, each input transcript also enters the graph as a single synthetic read, so that its structure is represented by an RCC and an RCC graph node like any read path, even where the read alignments alone would not recover it. Such a node can contain compatible RCCs of real reads, and real reads whose paths match the input transcript exactly join its RCC. The synthetic read itself is not counted as read evidence: a candidate is selected only if it represents at least one genuine read, so an input model that no read supports is not selected.

The trellis and its candidate paths are constructed once. During each iteration, LRAA selects the highest-scoring candidate that represents at least one RCC not yet represented by a selected candidate. The reads in the RCCs that the selected candidate represents are added to a cumulative exclusion set, and the remaining candidates are rescored by counting only the reads in their RCCs that are not in this set, and then reordered by score. Selection continues until every RCC is represented, no candidate paths remain, or the highest remaining score falls below the minimum path-support threshold (default, one read). The selected candidates that pass pairwise validation are then materialized as transcript models.

\subsection{Isoform filtering}\label{isoform-filtering}

\textbf{Initial filtering}: Following initial quantification, isoforms with less than one fractionally assigned read are removed, a floor applied again after expression-based filtering. Isoforms must be at least 200 bases long, except reference isoforms with assigned reads, and novel isoforms must have at least a sum of 2 fractionally assigned reads; single-exon isoforms, including those matching a reference single-exon transcript, count as novel for this criterion, and read support is recalculated after this filter to fractionally reassign reads from the filtered-out isoforms.

\textbf{Degradation product and alt TSS/PolyA isoform filtering}: LRAA compares isoforms of the same gene in pairs to remove truncated or degradation-derived products. Ignoring TSS and PolyA nodes, a candidate is eligible for pruning when its path has no more nodes than the comparison isoform's and is contained in or compatible with it. An eligible candidate is pruned when it has less than 20\% of the two isoforms' combined read support, or when its ends add no distinct evidence: it lacks both a TSS and a PolyA site, lacks a TSS and shares the comparison isoform's PolyA site, or lacks a PolyA site and shares its TSS. In the last two cases, a site with at most 20\% of the reads of the gene's strongest site of that terminus type counts as shared. A candidate lacking a TSS is also pruned when the comparison isoform lacks a PolyA site and their 3\p{} ends lie within 50 bp, and the comparison isoform then takes the candidate's PolyA site. A pruned candidate's reads are transferred to the comparison isoform, and read support is recalculated afterwards.

\textbf{Iterative expression-based filtering of lowly supported isoforms}: LRAA then removes weakly expressed isoforms within each gene, one round at a time. Each round re-estimates read assignments by EM and measures two fractions for every isoform: its share of the gene's fractionally assigned reads (isoform fraction), and the share of the gene's reads that are compatible with this isoform alone (unique-read fraction). Both minimums are 1\% by default and 0.5\% in HiFi mode. While more than 10 isoforms remain, isoforms below both minimums are removed together, lowest isoform fraction first, until 10 remain. Otherwise a round removes only the isoform with the lowest isoform fraction that falls below either minimum or is a novel isoform with fewer than two reads compatible with no other isoform of the gene. Rounds continue until no isoform can be removed.

\textbf{Reference isoforms in reference-guided runs}: An isoform whose intron chain exactly matches a reference transcript is exempt from these isoform-fraction and unique-read filters when it is the last remaining isoform with that intron chain and at least one read of the entire data set carries the whole chain. A reference isoform without such a read is kept only if it passes the filters on the reads assigned to it, as any other isoform; in long genes these are mostly partial reads that only this isoform can explain. A single-exon isoform counts as a reference isoform when it overlaps a same-strand single-exon reference transcript with reciprocal overlap of at least 80\%. 

\textbf{Monoexonic isoform filtering}: Single-exon isoforms are reconstructed in a separate pass that uses only unspliced reads. Where splicing dominates, unspliced reads in and around the spliced gene are treated as pre-mRNA or background rather than as evidence of separate transcripts; where splicing is a clear minority event, the unspliced reads are kept as possible evidence of a single-exon transcript. Accordingly, reads with at least half their length within an exon of a reconstructed multi-exon isoform are left out, and before exon segments are defined in the graph, the median intronic coverage is subtracted within introns up to the 80th length percentile, so that the low, near-uniform intronic background does not merge neighboring features into one exon segment. Neither step applies at an intron whose unspliced coverage exceeds 20 times its junction support: the flanking exons keep their reads and the intron's coverage is not subtracted. A single-exon isoform is kept only if it (i) has an expression of at least 1 TPM; (ii) shows evidence of a genuine 3\p{} end: an inferred PolyA site, a canonical polyadenylation signal 10--40 nt upstream of its 3\p{} end, or a supporting read with a poly(A) tail within 100 bp of it; (iii) is supported by reads that describe one contiguous transcript, with at least half of them overlapping a common base and covering on average at least 20\% of its length; and (iv) in single-cell runs, if novel, has reads from at least five cells. Single-exon isoforms that match a reference single-exon transcript (same strand, reciprocal overlap $\geq$80\%) and have at least one assigned read are exempt from (i), (ii) and (iv). Internally primed single-exon isoforms are removed as described below.

\textbf{Internal priming annotation and filter}: Isoforms are flagged as potentially internally primed when the strand-oriented 20-bp genomic sequence immediately downstream of the 3\p{} terminus contains either at least 12 adenines (thymines on the reverse strand) across the window or a run of at least six consecutive adenines or thymines within the eight bases nearest the terminus. These sequence patterns are consistent with oligo-dT priming at an internal genomic A-rich region rather than at a post-transcriptional PolyA tail. By default, flagged monoexonic isoforms are removed as likely internal-priming artifacts.

\textbf{Alignment-mismapping filter}: After final quantification, LRAA removes isoforms that are alignment artifacts of a much more highly expressed transcript, mainly false antisense isoforms assembled from reads assigned to the wrong strand. Two tests detect them. A multi-exon isoform is a strand mirror when it lies on the opposite strand of the other transcript, shares at least 50\% of its exonic bases with it, and has every splice site within 20 bp of that transcript's exon boundaries. An isoform is a sequence copy when its spliced sequence, on either strand, is at least 99\% identical over at least 85\% of its length to an isoform of a different gene, as found with minimap2 all-vs-all alignment of the isoform sequences; this also captures same-strand mismappings to paralogous loci, read-through and chimeric products. In either case the isoform is removed only when its expression is below 1\% of the matching transcript's, so well-expressed paralogs and genuine minor isoforms of the same gene are kept. Removed isoforms are dropped from the output and TPM values are rescaled over the remaining isoforms without re-quantification. This filter is applied when LRAA models new isoforms, hence not applied in quantification-only mode.

\subsection{Isoform terminal exon boundary refinement}\label{isoform-terminal-exon-boundary-refinement}

When an isoform end has no TSS or PolyA site call, LRAA places it from the reads the isoform represents. \textbf{5\p{} ends}: The 5\p{} end is placed by a tiered strategy that depends on the strength of the read evidence. First, the end goes to the largest pile-up of read 5\p{} ends within 2 bp of each other, with at least 2 reads (3 when more than 10 reads vote); the isoform's own reads vote first, and if they give no pile-up, the reads of all isoforms sharing its first intron vote. Without a pile-up, the end goes to the 20th percentile of the isoform's read 5\p{} ends when it has at least seven reads. With fewer reads, it goes to the most extended read, or in reference-guided runs to the annotated 5\p{} end of a reference transcript with the same intron chain if that extends further. An end placed more than 200 bp beyond the terminal exon's edge must have at least two read 5\p{} ends within 2 bp of it, or it stays within the exon. \textbf{3\p{} ends}: If at least seven reads end in the terminal exon, the end is set to the 90th percentile of their 3\p{} ends, counted along the transcript; otherwise it is left unchanged.

\subsection{Grouping isoforms to gene loci}\label{grouping-isoforms-to-gene-loci}

Reconstructed isoforms are assigned to gene loci: gene-level groups, each given a gene identifier, that approximate genes but are defined from the isoforms' shared structure. Grouping takes two steps. First, isoforms whose paths share at least one splice-graph node are joined into connected components. Each component is then split by linking isoforms that likely belong to the same gene: isoforms with identical intron chains are always placed together, and two other isoforms are linked when they share at least two introns that make up more than 20\% of the introns of the isoform with fewer introns, or when their exonic overlap covers at least 50\% of the shorter isoform. In components of up to 1,500 isoforms, Leiden community detection (resolution 0.2) on the graph of these links defines the gene loci. Larger components use faster single-linkage clustering of the same links, where an exonic overlap must also cover at least 20\% of the longer isoform unless it contains the shorter one.

\subsection{Read-to-isoform assignment}\label{read-to-isoform-assignment}

To quantify isoforms, labeled read paths must be assigned to compatible isoforms. Reads sharing identical splice structure (the same ordered sequence of splice-graph nodes, including TSS or PolyA nodes when available) are aggregated as a single labeled read path, a read compatibility class (RCC; defined above), with an associated read count. This aggregation is performed during RCC Graph construction and enables efficient assignment, since each unique splice-graph path is evaluated once. A path consists of the nodes the reads traverse from their first node to their last, so it reflects both the splice pattern and where the reads begin and end within the graph. Isoforms (reconstructed or provided as reference annotations) are similarly assigned labeled paths through the splice graph based on their structural representation within the splice graph.

LRAA performs transcriptome-based rescue of reads that cannot be assigned through the genome-derived splice-graph path. Under the default rescue procedure, the target database contains spliced sequences from multi-exon transcripts on the current contig and strand. Candidate rescue reads include reads with no path through the splice graph, reads whose alignments map only partially to it, gene-anchored reads whose paths are incompatible with all candidate isoforms (except in the streamed pass over all alignments; see Isoform quantification), and reads removed by the alignment percent-identity filter. LRAA realigns these reads to the target transcripts with minimap2 and projects accepted transcriptome alignments back onto genome-based splice-graph paths for read-to-isoform assignment. A transcriptome alignment is accepted when the target overlaps the read's genome alignment, at least 95\% of the read aligns as a single block with no indel of 30 bp or more (10 bp in HiFi mode), at an identity no lower than the alignment percent-identity threshold or the genome alignment's, and it aligns at least as much of the read length as the genome alignment.

During construction of labeled read paths, LRAA applies a constrained terminal-boundary snapping step integrating a TSS or PolyA site node not already present in the read path when it is directly adjacent to the read's terminal node, lies within 25 bp of the read end, and that read end has no soft clip. 

Each labeled read path is compared against the labeled paths of candidate isoforms, defined as the isoforms of any gene sharing at least one splice-graph node with the read path. Assignment proceeds through a cascading hierarchy of stringency levels, attempting the most stringent criteria first and progressively relaxing constraints until a compatible isoform is identified.

LRAA uses four compatibility tests, listed from most to least stringent. Two of them use the read overlap, the fraction of the read path's exonic length that lies in splice-graph nodes shared with the isoform.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\roman{enumi})}
\item
  \textbf{Exact:} the read and isoform paths are identical.
\item
  \textbf{Contained:} the read path appears in the isoform path as a consecutive run of nodes.
\item
  \textbf{Intron-compatible:} the read's introns are exactly the isoform's introns within the read's span, in the same order, and the read overlap is at least 75\%.
\item
  \textbf{Overlap-compatible:} from the first node the two paths share, they agree node for node, with no gaps, until one of them ends, and the read overlap is at least 75\%.
\end{enumerate}

A test is boundary-anchored when, in addition, any TSS or PolyA node on the read must be present and identical on the isoform. The cascade has nine steps in two phases. At each step, the read is tested against all candidate isoforms, the isoforms of every gene sharing at least one splice-graph node with the read path. The cascade stops at the first step at which at least one isoform is compatible, and the read is assigned to all isoforms compatible at that step (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:assign}}). Phase 1 keeps TSS and PolyA nodes in both read and isoform paths. Steps 1--4 apply tests (i)--(iv) with boundary anchoring, and step 5 applies test (iv) without anchoring, so a boundary present on only one path is tolerated, but conflicting boundary nodes still prevent compatibility. Phase 2 trims TSS and PolyA nodes from both paths and applies tests (i)--(iv) again as steps 6--9, so that a read whose TSS or PolyA node conflicts with those of every candidate isoform can still be assigned by its splice structure and range of exonic overlap.

For labeled read paths compatible with multiple isoforms, assignment ambiguity is resolved during quantification using relative expression estimates from EM and a strand-specific 3\p{}-agreement weight, as described below.

\subsection{Isoform quantification}\label{isoform-quantification}

Isoform abundances are estimated using an expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm that resolves ambiguous read assignments (building on EM-based frameworks described in \citep{li2011, bray2016, patro2017, kabza2024, zarejousheghani2025, xing2006}. Initial read-isoform compatibility and assignment follows the cascade described above, and EM (below) provides fractional assignments for reads to those isoforms according to maximum likelihood. 

Reads compatible with multiple isoforms are weighted according to agreement between the read and isoform 3\p{} ends, which suits the 3\p{}-anchored libraries commonly used for long-read RNA-seq. The weighting is applied by default and can be disabled (\texttt{-{}-no\_weight\_reads\_by\_3prime\_agreement}) for other library types. For read-compatibility class \(r\) and compatible isoform \(i\), the distance between the read's 3\p{} end and the isoform's 3\p{} end \(d_{ri}\) is calculated by traversing the isoform's splice-graph path inward from its strand-specific 3\p{} end to the first node also present in the read path and summing the complete lengths of intervening exon nodes. This is an exonic splice-graph path (spliced cDNA) distance rather than the genomic distance between the read and isoform endpoints. Let \(C_r\) be the set of isoforms compatible with \(r\), and let \(c\) be a pseudocount set to the configured alternative-PolyA window size (50 bp by default). LRAA calculates the unnormalized weight

\[w_{ri} \propto 1 - \frac{d_{ri} + c}{\left(\sum_{j \in C_r} d_{rj}\right) + |C_r|c}.\]

and then normalizes these weights across the isoforms in \(C_r\). This preserves preferential assignment to isoforms whose 3\p{} termini lie closer to the observed read end, while reducing overconfidence among nearby alternative PolyA sites.

Let \(\mathbf{p} = (p_1, \ldots, p_K)\) be the isoform proportions within a quantification unit (where \(\sum_{i=1}^K p_i = 1\)), and let \(R\) be the set of read compatibility classes (RCCs) assigned to it. A unit is a gene, or a set of genes that share at least one compatible read, such as a read-through model and the genes it spans, so reads compatible with isoforms of several genes are divided by a single EM. Each RCC \(r \in R\) corresponds to a unique labeled read path and aggregates \(n_r\) individual reads sharing that structure and assigned to the corresponding RCC. The likelihood of observing RCC \(r\) depends on the relative expression of its compatible isoforms and on its consistency with their 3\p{} ends:

\[P(r \mid \mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i: C_{ri}=1} p_i \cdot w_{ri}\]

where \(C_{ri} \in \{0,1\}\) indicates compatibility between RCC \(r\) and isoform \(i\), and \(w_{ri}\) is the 3\p{}-agreement weight defined above.

In the \textbf{expectation step}, posterior probabilities (i.e. fractions of each RCC assigned to each compatible isoform) are computed per RCC as the share of its likelihood contributed by each isoform:

\[\gamma_{ri} = \frac{p_i \cdot w_{ri}}{P(r \mid \mathbf{p})} = \frac{p_i \cdot w_{ri}}{\sum_{j: C_{rj}=1} p_j \cdot w_{rj}}\]

In the \textbf{maximization step}, LRAA updates each isoform proportion from its expected read count. On its own, EM tends to amplify small differences in distinguishing read support: over repeated updates, an isoform supported mainly by reads it shares with other isoforms, with few uniquely assignable reads, can lose nearly all of them to competing isoforms and be driven toward zero abundance, even though those reads do not rule it out. To keep unresolved read compatibility from being taken as evidence that an isoform is not expressed, LRAA adds a pseudocount to each isoform's expected read count that scales with its ambiguous read support. Let \(A_i\) be the total read count across ambiguous RCCs whose compatibility sets include isoform \(i\). The pseudocount added to isoform \(i\) is \(\alpha_i = \alpha A_i,\) where \(\alpha\), the pseudocount fraction, is the share of the isoform's ambiguous read count that is added (1\% by default in final quantification). Each ambiguous RCC contributes its full read count to \(A_i\) for every compatible isoform. If isoform \(i\) has no ambiguous RCC support, then \(A_i = 0\) and \(\alpha_i = 0\).

Given the fractional assignments from the expectation step, the adjusted expected read count for isoform \(i\), its expected read count plus its pseudocount, is

\[\widetilde{N}_i^{(t+1)}=\sum_{r:\,i\in C_r}n_r\gamma_{ri}^{(t)}+\alpha_i,\]

where \(C_r\) is the set of isoforms compatible with RCC \(r\), \(n_r\) is the number of reads represented by that RCC, \(\gamma_{ri}^{(t)}\) is its fractional assignment to isoform \(i\) at iteration \(t\), and \(\alpha_i\) is its pseudocount.

The updated isoform proportion is obtained by normalizing this value across all isoforms:

\[p_i^{(t+1)}=\frac{\widetilde{N}_i^{(t+1)}}{\displaystyle\sum_k\widetilde{N}_k^{(t+1)}}.\]

By default the proportions are estimated from the thinned alignments used to build the splice graph (see Normalization of read coverage), each read weighted by the inverse of its sampling probability, so \(n_r\) and \(A_i\) are weighted read counts. All alignments of the filtered BAM are then streamed, and each read is divided among its compatible isoforms by one expectation step at the estimated proportions. Reported read counts are the sums of these fractions.

During draft transcript assembly and iterative filtering, LRAA sets \(\alpha = 0\) and permits up to 250 EM iterations. At this stage, abundance estimates help determine which candidate isoforms are retained, and the pseudocount is deliberately left out. The EM behavior that the pseudocount counters in final quantification is used here instead: candidates with minimal distinguishing read support are driven toward zero abundance and removed by filtering, rather than retained only because they are compatible with many ambiguous reads.

Once the transcript set has been fixed, this model-selection concern no longer applies, and during final quantification and quantification-only mode LRAA sets \(\alpha = 0.01\) by default, with the same 250-iteration limit. Regularization (via the pseudocount) then preserves nonzero abundance estimates for isoforms whose reads remain difficult to assign uniquely without changing which transcripts are reconstructed.

In each EM, iteration stops early when the Euclidean norm of the change between consecutive isoform-proportion vectors falls below 1e-6, and otherwise after 250 iterations. LRAA reports for each isoform the summed fractional read assignments, the number of reads compatible with that isoform alone, the isoform fraction (its share of the gene's assigned reads), and TPM, computed as assigned reads per million reads assigned to all reported isoforms.

For genomic contigs such as the mitochondrial genome, we avoid reconstructing isoforms, as their read coverage can be very high while their known transcript complexity is low, so isoform reconstruction would consume substantial computing resources for little benefit. For such contigs LRAA offers a simplified mode, -\/-oversimplify, applied by default to the mitochondrial contigs, that bypasses graph-based assignment and EM. In quantification-only or reference-guided runs with supplied transcript models, each read is assigned to the model having the greatest exonic base-pair overlap. In annotation-free discovery, LRAA instead creates a single-exon pseudo-transcript for each strand of the contig and assigns all such reads to that pseudo-transcript.

\subsection{LRAA single-cell RNA-seq automated workflow}\label{lraa-single-cell-rna-seq-automated-workflow}

LRAA-singlecell is implemented as a Workflow Description Language (WDL) workflow, enabling portable execution across cloud platforms, high-performance computing clusters, and local compute environments. Unless otherwise noted, all isoform discovery and quantification steps apply the LRAA algorithms described above, with parameters adapted for single-cell data characteristics as specified below.

The workflow runs in one of two modes. Basic mode treats all cells together: it pools the reads of all cells into a single pseudobulk for isoform discovery and quantification, builds gene-, isoform-, and splice-pattern-by-cell count matrices, filters cells, and clusters them by gene-level expression. Cluster-guided mode extends basic mode: it uses the basic-mode isoforms and cell clusters, or user-supplied ones, to rerun isoform discovery separately on the reads of each cluster, to recover isoforms that are low in abundance in the pooled data but enriched within specific cell populations, which pooled discovery can miss or filter out as minor isoforms. The per-cluster isoforms are then merged into one catalog, against which the reads of all cells are quantified. Either mode can discover isoforms de novo or guided by a reference annotation, or, in quantification-only mode, skip discovery and quantify against a supplied annotation.

\subsection{Input and preprocessing}\label{input-and-preprocessing-1}

LRAA-singlecell requires coordinate-sorted BAM files of long-read single-cell RNA-seq data aligned to a reference genome, the reference genome in FASTA format, and cell barcode annotations embedded in BAM read tags. Cell barcodes are read from a configurable BAM tag (default: CB, following the 10x Genomics convention), and unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) are read from a separate tag (default: XM).

Cluster-guided mode uses cell cluster assignments, either produced by the basic-mode steps within the workflow or supplied as a tab-separated file mapping cell barcodes to cluster identifiers from external clustering analyses. Reference gene annotations in GTF format are optional for discovery modes but required for quantification-only mode.

\subsection{Basic mode workflow}\label{basic-mode-workflow}

\subsubsection{Initial isoform discovery}\label{initial-isoform-discovery}

In basic mode, LRAA first performs transcript isoform discovery on the complete dataset by pooling all single-cell reads into a unified pseudobulk analysis. This initial discovery step constructs a splice graph from all reads combined, treating the dataset as conventional bulk RNA-seq to establish an initial catalog of expressed isoforms across the entire cell population. During pooled quantification, LRAA writes one tracking row for each assigned read--isoform pair. Each row contains the gene and transcript identifiers, the transcript splice-pattern hash, the number of exons in the isoform, the assigned read-path identifier, the read name, the fractional assignment, the 3\p{}-end agreement weight used during quantification, and flags for whether the read is compatible with this isoform alone and whether it reproduces the isoform's intron chain. For single-cell reads whose alignments carry both the configured cell-barcode and UMI tags, those values are encoded in the reported read name. Reads without an isoform assignment are not included. The tracking file is used to construct downstream gene-, isoform-, and splice-pattern-by-cell count matrices.

\subsubsection{Single-cell sparse matrix construction}\label{single-cell-sparse-matrix-construction}

For each unique cell barcode in the dataset, fractional read assignments are summed at three hierarchical levels (these are UMI counts when the input BAM holds one read per UMI): (1) gene-level counts, summing reads assigned to any isoform of a gene; (2) isoform-level counts, summing reads assigned to specific transcript isoforms; and (3) splice pattern-level counts, grouping reads by unique splice junction combinations regardless of transcript identity. Splice patterns are encoded as hash codes derived from the ordered sequence of intron coordinates (as inspired by Isosceles \citep{kabza2024}), enabling detection of alternative splicing events independent of full-length isoform reconstruction. Monoexonic isoforms lack an intron chain and therefore retain transcript-specific identifiers.

\subsubsection{Cell quality filtering}\label{cell-quality-filtering}

To distinguish genuine cells from empty droplets and low-quality cells, a filtering step applied before clustering (on by default) uses the DropletUtils emptyDrops statistical framework \citep{lun2019}. The gene-level sparse matrix is used to model the ambient RNA profile from low-count barcodes and to compute false discovery rates (FDR) for each barcode under the null hypothesis that observed counts arise solely from ambient contamination. Barcodes with FDR ≤ 0.01 (default) are retained as high-confidence cells.

\subsubsection{Cell clustering}\label{cell-clustering}

Cells are clustered using a Seurat \citep{hao2024} based workflow implemented in R that applies standard single-cell normalization, dimensionality reduction, and graph-based clustering to the filtered gene-level sparse matrix. The workflow follows conventional Seurat practices:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  Genes expressed in fewer than a specified minimum number of cells (default: 10) are filtered to remove rare features.
\item
  Cells expressing fewer than a specified minimum number of genes (default: 1000) or at least a maximum threshold of mitochondrial content (default: 20\%) are removed as low quality.
\item
  Gene-level counts are log-normalized to 10,000 reads per cell.
\item
  The top most highly variable genes (default: 2000) are identified via variance-stabilizing transformation.
\item
  Data are scaled with the number of detected genes, total counts, and percent mitochondrial content regressed out, and principal component analysis is performed on the variable genes. The top principal components (default: 12) are retained for downstream neighbor finding and visualization.
\item
  A shared nearest neighbor (SNN) graph is constructed over those components and clustered at resolution (default: 0.6) using the default Seurat FindClusters algorithm (Louvain \citep{blondel2008}).
\item
  Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) \citep{mcinnes2018} is computed for visualization.
\end{enumerate}

Cell clustering results are exported as a tab-separated assignment file mapping each cell barcode to its cluster identifier. This file serves as input for the downstream cluster-guided LRAA workflow.

\subsection{LRAA cell cluster-guided mode workflow}\label{lraa-cell-cluster-guided-mode-workflow}

\subsubsection{Per-cluster isoform discovery}\label{per-cluster-isoform-discovery}

Following cell clustering, either from basic mode or from provided cluster assignments, the reads are partitioned by cell cluster according to their cell barcodes, giving one pseudobulk read set per cluster; reads from barcodes not assigned to a cluster are left out of per-cluster discovery. Each cluster's reads are processed independently through the complete LRAA isoform discovery pipeline. The initial GTF (from basic mode, or supplied by the user) serves as the annotation for reference-guided LRAA isoform identification in each cluster-specific BAM. Only the initial isoforms that the pseudobulk run marked as matching a reference transcript are treated as reference isoforms in the cluster runs, using the full-chain read counts of the pooled data; the other initial isoforms are novel there and must meet the novel-isoform read requirements within each cluster. Each cluster run counts supporting cells only among that cluster's barcodes.

\subsubsection{Cell cluster GTF merging for a consensus isoform catalog}\label{cell-cluster-gtf-merging-for-a-consensus-isoform-catalog}

The per-cluster GTFs and the initial pseudobulk GTF are merged into one isoform catalog by a further LRAA run that treats each input isoform as a synthetic read and reassembles them with the same splice-graph and path-selection steps used for reads. Isoforms that share their splice junctions and differ only slightly at their ends collapse into a single transcript, with gene assignments unified across clusters, whereas isoforms found in only one cluster are kept. Including the pseudobulk GTF keeps its isoforms in the catalog when no single cluster has the depth to rediscover them. The TSS and PolyA sites of the input isoforms are carried into the merged catalog with their per-cluster read support, and a merged isoform without a TSS that lies within another isoform of its gene is absorbed into it.

\subsubsection{Final quantification and single-cell matrix generation}\label{final-quantification-and-single-cell-matrix-generation}

After the merge, all input reads are quantified together against the merged isoform catalog in a single final quantification. As in basic mode, the read-to-isoform assignments, each carrying its cell barcode, are summed into gene-, isoform-, and splice-pattern-by-cell count matrices, and per-cluster pseudobulk expression is obtained by summing the assignments of each cluster's cells.

\subsubsection{Reference annotation gene symbol integration}\label{reference-annotation-gene-symbol-integration}

To facilitate interpretation and integration with reference annotations, an optional gene symbol incorporation step annotates LRAA-discovered isoforms with official gene symbols from a reference GTF. This procedure uses gffcompare \citep{pertea2020} to compare the LRAA GTF to a reference annotation (e.g., GENCODE \citep{mudge2025}), identify matching or overlapping loci, and transfer gene symbols to LRAA transcripts based on structural similarity. After gene symbol assignment, all single-cell sparse matrices (gene, isoform, and splice pattern levels) are updated to incorporate gene symbols into the LRAA-assigned feature identifiers (genes, isoforms, and splice patterns).

\subsection{LRAA single cell workflow architecture and optimization}\label{lraa-single-cell-workflow-architecture-and-optimization}

The workflow leverages parallel execution at multiple stages. Each LRAA stage (initial discovery, per-cluster discovery and final quantification) is scattered across contigs and within-contig chunks, and in cluster-guided mode one chunk plan is used for every stage. LRAA isoform discovery is restricted to the nuclear genome. Mitochondrial genes are quantified based on reference gene annotations or simply quantified as reads per mitochondrial genome strand for estimating cellular mitochondrial read content in annotation-free mode. Per-cell-cluster discovery in cluster-guided mode executes clusters in parallel via WDL scatter blocks.

To support iterative analyses and avoid recomputation, the workflow accepts optional precomputed inputs. Users can provide a precomputed initial GTF and tracking files to skip initial discovery, or precomputed cluster assignments to skip cell filtering and clustering. This design enables expert users to provide curated cell cluster assignments for downstream cluster-guided isoform identification and quantification.

The workflow produces stage-specific outputs, including intermediate files for quality control and debugging. Depending on the mode selected and on which stages are bypassed by precomputed inputs, these include the initial and merged GTF files, the splice-pattern-collapsed GTF, the final TSS and PolyA BED files, the TSS cap-calibration report, per-cluster BAMs, tracking files and pseudobulk expression matrices packaged as archives, the per-cluster read partition, sparse single-cell matrices, Seurat objects and UMAP visualizations, and gene symbol mappings.

\subsection{Single cell / nuclei analyses}\label{single-cell-nuclei-analyses}

Single cell and nuclei analyses were performed using Seurat v5 \citep{hao2024}. For cell clustering, gene expression quantification was performed using LRAA in quantification-only mode along with GENCODE v47 reference annotations and minimap2 alignments of Kinnex reads to the GRCh38 reference genome. Gene-level sparse quantification matrices were loaded into Seurat and genes and cells were filtered requiring genes expressed in at least 10 cells/nuclei, at least 500 genes expressed per cell/nuclei, and a maximum mitochondrial gene expression of 15\% for cells and 5\% for nuclei. Each cluster was assigned the cell type CAS \citep{williams2025} predicted most often among its cells (counting cells whose top prediction scored above 0.5). Two FTD frontal cortex clusters were relabelled from marker genes: cluster 12 as leptomeningeal cells (predicted astrocytes; \emph{FOXC1}, \emph{FOXC2}, \emph{SLC47A1} and \emph{MGP} high, \emph{GFAP}, \emph{AQP4} and \emph{SLC1A2} low) and cluster 13 as pericytes (predicted vascular leptomeningeal cells; \emph{PDGFRB}, \emph{RGS5} and \emph{NOTCH3} high, the fibroblast markers \emph{CEMIP} and \emph{PDGFRA} low). For the FTD nuclei, marker genes were identified with Seurat's `FindAllMarkers' and pairwise differential expression between clusters with `FindMarkers', on the gene matrix of the reference-guided, cluster-guided LRAA run and the clusters above. For the FTD frontal cortex clusters (\textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:6}b}), clusters were related by hierarchical clustering (complete linkage) of the number of genes differentially expressed between each pair of clusters (adjusted $p \leq 0.05$, unadjusted $p < 10^{-5}$, $|\log_2 \mathrm{FC}| \geq 2$), and marker genes were those upregulated in a cluster against all other nuclei (adjusted $p < 10^{-5}$, $\log_2 \mathrm{FC} \geq 2$). Other Seurat analyses including generating UMAPs, clustering cells or nuclei and parameter settings are available in \textbf{Supplementary Code}. Functional enrichment analyses were performed using GProfiler \citep{kolberg2020}.

\subsection{Differential isoform usage testing}\label{differential-isoform-usage-testing}

Differential usage between cell clusters was tested with DEXSeq \citep{anders2012} on pseudo-replicates, the approach Sierra \citep{patrick2020} and SCAPE \citep{zhou2022} take to polyadenylation site usage in single-cell data, using the reference-guided, cluster-guided LRAA isoform set. Two kinds of feature were tested: (1) splice patterns, for alternative splicing, with the LRAA read counts of isoforms sharing an intron chain summed per splice pattern (unspliced isoforms excluded); and (2) TSS and PolyA sites, for alternative transcription start and end usage, counting reads by their 5\p{} or 3\p{} ends within 25 nt of LRAA's final sites. Features without a single gene symbol were excluded, as were PolyA sites LRAA labeled as potentially internally primed. Each site switch was classified from the splicing of the reads at its two sites as tandem (the two sites lie on the same terminal exon, with the same splicing, and differ only in where the transcript starts or ends) or coupled with alternative splicing (the two sites differ in splicing, including alternative first or last exons).

Testing had two stages: a single test across all clusters first identified genes whose feature usage differs among clusters, and these genes were then compared between each pair of clusters to find which clusters differ and which features change. The cells of each cluster were partitioned at random into three pseudo-replicates, and counts were summed per feature, with features standing in for DEXSeq's exon bins and genes for its groups. For each feature, DEXSeq fitted negative binomial models over all clusters with at least 30 cells and compared them by a likelihood-ratio test:
\begin{align*}
\text{full:}\quad & \text{count} \sim \text{pseudoReplicate} + \text{featureIndicator} + \text{cluster}{:}\text{featureIndicator},\\
\text{reduced:}\quad & \text{count} \sim \text{pseudoReplicate} + \text{featureIndicator},
\end{align*}
where the counts are the reads of the feature (featureIndicator 1, DEXSeq's ``this'' bin) and the summed reads of the gene's other tested features of the same type (featureIndicator 0, ``others''). The interaction term lets the feature's share of its gene's counts differ between clusters, so the test asks whether usage differs among clusters, whatever the gene's expression. This is DEXSeq's standard design, here fitted across all clusters at once rather than to two groups at a time, as in Sierra and SCAPE. Gene-level q-values came from perGeneQValue, and stageR \citep{vandenberge2017} confirmed the features carrying the change. Before testing, features were filtered on their counts summed per cluster. A feature was kept if it made up at least 10\% of its gene's counts in at least one cluster where the gene had at least 20 counts, so that it is a substantial alternative somewhere, and if it had at least 10 counts summed over all tested clusters. A gene was tested only if at least two of its features passed.  The pseudo-replicates come from a single sample, so they capture cell-to-cell sampling variation rather than variation between donors, and the P values are optimistic in absolute terms \citep{squair2021}. We therefore did not rely on P values alone. The partitioning of cells across pseudo-replicates is random, so the durability of the test was evaluated by repeating it in five trials, each with a different random seed, and a gene was called significant when q $<$ 0.05 in at least four of them; high-confidence events further required a minimum effect size and full-length read support (below), and events were ranked by effect size.

Significant genes were then compared between each pair of clusters on that pair's pseudo-replicates from the first partitioning, with Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment over all pairwise tests. A differential usage event was a gene and cluster pair with a feature at adjusted P $<$ 0.05, a change of at least 0.2 in its usage (its share of the gene's tested counts, \(\pi\)), and at least 20 gene counts in each of the two clusters. Each event pairs the feature with the largest significant gain with the feature whose usage fell most, and is reciprocal when, in counts per million, the gained feature rose and the lost feature fell, each at least 1.5-fold. High-confidence events were reciprocal, had at least five unique full-length (FSM) reads for each of the two features, and, for site events, passed quality checks. For both TSS and PolyA events, the two sites had to lie at least 30 bp apart, and neither could be carried only by single-exon isoforms. A TSS event was excluded when the gained TSS lay downstream of the lost one and no isoform starting there had five unique FSM reads, as expected for 5\p{}-truncated reads. A PolyA event was excluded when either site looked internally primed, judged by the number of A's among the 20 genomic bases past the site: at least 12, or more than 7 at a site with neither a polyadenylation signal nor a cluster in the PolyASite~2.0 database found in at least 10\% of the database's samples. Each site of a TSS switch (reference-guided, cluster-guided sites) was matched to the nearest FANTOM5 CAGE peak \citep{forrest2014} within 25 nt on the same strand. High-confidence events are listed in \textbf{Supplementary Table~1}, and all events are available in the \textbf{Supplementary Code}.

\subsection{Evaluating transcript start and end sites}\label{evaluating-transcript-start-and-end-sites}

\subsubsection{Agreement with TSS and PolyA atlases}\label{agreement-with-tss-and-polya-atlases}

LRAA's de novo TSS and PolyA sites (pseudobulk and cluster-guided runs) were compared with FANTOM5 CAGE peaks (hg38) \citep{forrest2014} and PolyASite~2.0 clusters (GRCh38) \citep{herrmann2020} with \texttt{bedtools closest} \citep{quinlan2010} on the same strand; a site agreed when it lay inside a peak or cluster. The same sites placed at random within the LRAA transcript spans (\texttt{bedtools shuffle}) gave the agreement expected by chance (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:siteatlas}}a,b). For sites within 100 nt of an atlas entry, we also recorded the signed offset from its representative point, the CAGE peak summit or the PolyASite cleavage site (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:siteatlas}}c).

\subsubsection{5\p{} completeness and the cap signature}\label{five-prime-completeness-and-the-cap-signature}

We measured 5\p{} completeness as how often a read ending at a transcript's PolyA site reaches back to the transcript's TSS. We first selected genes whose single LRAA TSS site agrees with a GENCODE v22 transcript (the site lies in the transcript's first exon or within 50 nt upstream of it), taking for each gene the transcript whose full intron chain the most reads reproduce. We then took the reads that are splice-compatible with that transcript (all of their introns are among its introns) and that end within 50 nt of the gene's most common read 3\p{} end, and recorded how far each extends toward the TSS; a read starting within 50 nt of the TSS counts as full length. A cell type's completeness curve (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:tss5prime}}b) shows the share of these reads extending at least a given distance from the 3\p{} end, averaged over genes at least that long with at least 20 such reads.

Reads that begin at a capped 5\p{} end often carry an extra, untemplated G, seen as a soft-clipped G at the start of the alignment \citep{wulf2019, cumbie2015, moody2022}. We compared the share of reads with this G between reads starting at the TSS and reads starting more than 100 nt downstream of it, and at each pile-up of read 5\p{} ends called by LRAA's library pre-pass (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:tss5prime}}e), excluding reads preceded by a G in the genome (see above). To compare cell types, we computed each cell type's share of capped reads (by LRAA's definition above) among its informative spliced reads, and the share of its TSS peaks that are uncapped (below the library's Otsu threshold). Because the number of peaks called grows with read depth, these peaks were called after subsampling every cell type to the read count of the smallest one.

\subsubsection{Internal TSSs of incomplete splice match isoforms}\label{internal-tss-of-ism-isoforms}

Some isoforms look like 5\p{}-truncated copies of a longer isoform yet are transcribed from a genuine internal promoter. To find such cases and judge whether their starts have independent transcriptional support, we examined one type of incomplete splice match (ISM) isoform, which we call a strict ISM: an isoform S that is an ISM of a longer isoform L, shares L's 3\p{} terminus (within 50 nt), and has its TSS within a downstream exon of L, keeping at least one of L's introns. We searched for such pairs of S and L within each LRAA v0.51.0 model set: for PBMCs and FTD, the basic and cluster-guided sets of the de novo and reference-guided runs, and for BT474, K562, HG002 and UHRR, the de novo and reference-guided runs. Because the same internal TSS often appears in several of a library's model sets with 5\p{} ends a few nucleotides apart, S models with the same intron chain whose 5\p{} ends lie within 25 nt of each other were grouped as one internal TSS, counted once per library (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:ism}}a).

Each internal TSS was judged on the reads using S's first intron, at a site taken as their most common start within 5 nt of the TSS, $\pm$5 nt. A pile-up required at least 5 reads at the site, making up at least 50\% of the reads starting within 50 nt and at least 10\% of the reads over S's first intron. Cap evidence required at least 5 informative reads, at least 2 capped reads and at least 10\% capped, by LRAA's per-read rule (above) applied to these reads. Atlas evidence was a same-strand FANTOM5 CAGE \citep{forrest2014} or refTSS v4.1 \citep{abugessaisa2019} peak within 10 nt. As background, we drew 10 random positions per TSS in the same L exon, at least 10 nt from its edges and more than 50 nt from the site (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:ism}}b). A TSS was considered canonical if located within 50 nt of the TSS of a MANE Select or Ensembl canonical coding transcript in GENCODE v47.

To determine the effect of each internal TSS on the encoded protein, L's coding sequence came from the GENCODE v47 transcript with L's intron chain, so that L's start codon and reading frame were the annotated ones, or otherwise from the complete ORF that pyTD v0.4.0 \citep{pytransdecoder} predicted on L, as in the proteoform analysis (below). S implied an N-terminal truncation when L's start codon was lost and the first downstream in-frame ATG in S kept L's reading frame. Such a TSS was counted when it had pile-up, cap and atlas evidence, was not canonical, and at least 5 reads crossed the L intron that S lacks (\textbf{Supplementary Table 2}). For the examples (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:ism}}d--g), the regions and domains lost with the shortened N-terminus were taken from the feature annotations of the gene's reviewed UniProt entry \citep{uniprot2025}, with the shorter protein placed on the canonical UniProt sequence by matching its first 25 amino acids; features ending before the shorter protein's start were counted as lost, and features spanning it as partial.

To compare isoform reconstruction methods, we applied the same definitions to the de novo models of LRAA, IsoQuant, StringTie, Bambu, ESPRESSO, IsoSeq and Mandalorion for each bulk library, taking L from the same tool's models or from GENCODE v47 and keeping, as above, starts that were not canonical and had at least 5 reads crossing the L intron that S lacks. TSS sites were defined on the pooled models of all tools and GENCODE, so that the tools were judged at the same sites. A start was supported when it had pile-up, cap and atlas evidence, fragment-like when it had a pile-up of at least 5 informative reads but neither the cap signature nor an atlas peak, and otherwise not judged (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:ism}}c). Because LRAA uses the cap signature to call TSSs, agreement with CAGE and refTSS peaks is the part of this comparison that is independent of LRAA.

\subsection{Novel Proteoform Detection}\label{novel-proteoform-detection}

We searched genome-wide for proteoforms encoded by the reference-guided, cluster-guided LRAA isoforms classified as FSM, ISM, NIC or NNIC. Open reading frames (ORFs) were predicted with pyTD v0.4.0 \citep{pytransdecoder} on transcript sequences extracted with gffread \citep{pertea2020}, keeping the single best ORF per transcript, and complete ORFs (with both start and stop codons) were analyzed at minimum lengths of 50, 100 and 200 amino acids. The splice junctions (donor-acceptor pairs) of each isoform were compared against all GENCODE v47 junctions \citep{gencodendb}, and a novel junction with both its donor and acceptor in the CDS was classified as a Novel Junction. An internal exon absent from the gene's reference annotation but using known splice donor and acceptor sites of that gene represents retained intronic sequence, and was classified as Intron Retention when it overlapped the CDS.

For protein-level counts, isoforms encoding identical protein sequences were counted once. A protein was a Reference Matched Proteoform if any isoform encoding it had no novel event, and a Novel Proteoform otherwise, with its event type (Novel Junction, Intron Retention or both) taken from one of its isoforms chosen at random (fixed seed).

The 3D structures of AIF1 proteoforms were modeled using ColabFold v1.5.5 (AlphaFold2 coupled with MMseqs2) with default parameters \citep{mirdita2022}. Protein domains were predicted by InterProScan v5.76-107.0 \citep{jones2014}. Structural superimposition and visualization were performed using UCSF ChimeraX v1.10.1 \citep{meng2023}.

\subsection{Generation of Simulated Long-Read Transcriptomic Data of Mouse and Arabidopsis}\label{generation-of-simulated-long-read-transcriptomic-data-of-mouse-and-arabidopsis}

To establish a robust ground truth for benchmarking isoform identification and quantification tools, we generated simulated long-read datasets for mouse (\emph{Mus musculus}) and Arabidopsis (\emph{Arabidopsis thaliana}). Input transcript relative abundances were empirically derived from high-depth RNA-seq data (Mouse: SRR203276, \citep{grabherr2011}; Arabidopsis: SRR3664432,\citep{li2016} and quantified using Salmon (v0.13.1) \citep{patro2017} against GENCODE vM32 \citep{gencodendc} and Araport11 \citep{cheng2017} annotations, respectively. These abundance profiles were normalized to a target sequencing depth of 20 million reads and integrated into a customized version of IsoSeqSim (v0.2) (available at \citep{isoseqsimnd}, forked from \citep{wangnd}). This specific fork was modified to allow the simulator to initialize using a user-provided count matrix, ensuring simulated reads precisely reflected the empirical expression dynamics of the source datasets.

With IsoSeqSim, we simulated a gradient of four error profiles modeled on PacBio sequencing artifacts by adjusting the rates of substitutions (es), deletions (ed), and insertions (ei). This spanned a perfect control (0\% error) and three error-prone datasets: low Error (1.6\% total; es: 0.4\%, ed: 0.6\%, ei: 0.6\%), medium Error (5.0\% total; es: 1.7\%, ed: 1.1\%, ei: 2.2\%), and high Error (8.5\% total; es: 2.7\%, ed: 1.8\%, ei: 4.0\%). Read incompleteness was modeled using IsoSeqSim-provided PacBio Sequel settings and dedicated parameters for 3\p{} (c3) and 5\p{} (c5) end completeness. Reads were mapped to their respective reference genomes (GRCm39 and TAIR10) using minimap2 (v2.28) \citep{li2018} with splice-aware presets (-ax splice) and processed with samtools (v1.15.1) \citep{li2009} into sorted BAM files.

To evaluate performance in reference-guided mode, we generated a reduced reference by randomly retaining exactly one isoform per gene from the full annotations. We then defined the expressed ground truth by filtering the original annotations for transcripts with non-zero abundance in our simulated data. By identifying the intersection between the single-isoform reduced reference and the expressed ground truth, we effectively partitioned the datasets into known isoforms (expressed and present in the reduced reference) and novel isoforms (expressed but artificially removed). This design allows for a rigorous evaluation of a tool\textquotesingle s ability to reconstruct missing isoforms while quantifying the whole transcriptome, by providing the reduced reference to the tools. Unlike the entire chromosome hold-out \citep{chen2023} or random isoform subsampling \citep{pardopalacios2024b}, our approach creates a skeleton reference. By reducing transcript complexity while strictly maintaining gene loci, we were able to test how well discovery tools can reconstruct alternative splicing and novel isoforms against a constrained ground truth.

\subsection{Generation of Long-read Transcriptomic Data of SIRVs and Human Cell Lines}\label{generation-of-long-read-transcriptomic-data-of-sirvs-and-human-cell-lines}

Four total RNA samples were leveraged to generate long-read transcriptomic data: Universal Human Reference RNA (UHRR), GM24385, K562, and BT474. cDNA synthesis and library preparation were performed following the standard PacBio Kinnex full-length RNA library protocol. For each cDNA synthesis reaction, 300 ng of RNA sample was spiked with 0.09 ng of one of the three SIRV Isoform Mixes (E0, E1, or E2) from SIRV-Set 1, targeting 1\% of SIRV reads per RNA sample. cDNA synthesis reactions were performed in duplicate and cleaned with 3 serial 1.3X SMRTbell bead cleanups prior to amplification and concatenation. 24 final Kinnex libraries (comprising four distinct cell lines spiked with three different SIRV concentrations; each condition was performed in duplicate) were generated and pooled for sequencing. Each library was sequenced on the PacBio Revio system across 4 flow cells, yielding approximately 4 million reads per sample.

To evaluate isoform reconstruction accuracy using spike-in controls, deconcatenated HiFi poly(A) tail-trimmed reads were aligned to the GRCh38 reference genome supplemented with SIRV Set 1 sequences (\url{https://www.lexogen.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/SIRV_Set1_Norm_Sequences_20210507.zip}) using minimap2 (v2.28) \citep{li2018}. Alignment was performed in splice-aware mode (-ax splice:hq). Post-alignment, reads were partitioned based on their mapping targets (SIRV contigs versus human ones), yielding sorted and indexed BAM files for independent evaluation. We defined the isoform quantification ground truth of spike-in by redistributing the total count of mapped SIRV reads across individual isoforms according to their manufacturer-defined stoichiometric ratios. Similar to the simulated datasets, we also generated benchmark references to evaluate reference-guided isoform discovery by parsing the GRCh38.gencode.v39 and SIRV annotation files to retain exactly one randomly selected transcript per gene.

\subsection{Generation of Long-read Transcriptomic Data of MORFs}\label{generation-of-long-read-transcriptomic-data-of-morfs}

\subsection{MORF plasmid amplification and electroporation}\label{morf-plasmid-amplification-and-electroporation}

Multiplexed Overexpression of Regulatory Factors (MORF) pooled plasmid library (comprising 1,836 genes encoded by 3,548 isoforms) was a gift from Feng Zhang (Addgene \#1000000218) \citep{joung2023}. Plasmid libraries were amplified by electroporation of 0.5 μg library into Endura electrocompetent cells (cat \#60242-2) using BioRad Gene Pulser Xcell Total System (cat \#1652660) with the following settings: voltage 2.5 kV, capacitance 25 μF, resistance 200 Ω, cuvette 2 mm. Cells were immediately recovered by adding 1 mL of room temperature recovery media (cat \#60242-2) and moving to a Celltreat recovery tube (cat \#229475) and incubated on a shaker at 30°C for 20 minutes. Recovered cells were mixed into 500 mL LB media containing Carbenicillin (100 μg/ml; from Broad Institute SQM) and incubated for 14 hours at 30°C. Serial dilutions were plated onto LB agar plates containing Carbenicillin (100 μg/mL; from Broad Institute SQM) and incubated at 30°C for 18 hrs to quantify library diversity (55 million CFU). Plasmid purification was carried out using ZymoPURE II Plasmid Midiprep Kit (Zymo; D4201), splitting the 500 mL liquid cultures into eight separate purifications, followed by endotoxin removal (1800 μL at 276 ng/μL).

\subsubsection{MORF plasmid QC via long read PacBio sequencing}\label{morf-plasmid-qc-via-long-read-pacbio-sequencing}

To verify the integrity of the MORF plasmid library, full length MORFs were amplified off the plasmid DNA using primers flanking the EF1a promoter and WPRE containing ``MARS'' and ``Venus'' overhangs (DBO61 \& DBO62; IDT) using the following conditions: 25 μL of 2x KAPA Hi-Fi Uracil ready mix (Roche), 5 μL of MORF plasmid library (50 ng/μL) , 2.5 ul of 10 uM forward primer (DBO61; IDT), 2.5 μL of 10 μM rev primer (DBO62; IDT), and 15 μL H\textsubscript{2}O to bring final volume to 50 μL. Reactions were amplified using the following cycling conditions: 98°C for 3 min, followed by 6 cycles (determined by qPCR) of 98°C for 20 s, 64°C for 30 s and 72°C for 8 min, followed by a final 72°C extension for 10 min. The amplified libraries were cleaned using 1.8× SPRIselect beads (Beckman Coulter B23318), eluted into a 25 μL elution buffer and quantified with Qubit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Q32851). The resulting amplicons were prepared for long-read sequencing using the PacBio 8-mer concatenation MAS-seq kit. Sequencing was performed on the PacBio Revio system.

\subsubsection{MORF plasmid QC Analysis}\label{morf-plasmid-qc-analysis}

PacBio HiFi reads were first segmented at adapter positions using skera (v1.2.0) and aligned to the MORF ORF insert reference (containing EF1a-ORF-Barcode-WPRE elements) using minimap2 (v2.28). To evaluate structural completeness, we examined minimap2 read alignments for corresponding reference transcript coverage. We further validated the sequences by performing a similarity search using BLAST+ (v2.15.0), querying the synthetic MORF sequences against the GENCODE v39 reference \citep{gencodend}. We applied a stringent dual-threshold filter, retaining only those MORFs that exhibited both a sequence identity and a mean query coverage of \textgreater= 99\%. This process retrieved 2,923 high-confidence isoforms from 1,566 human genes, providing a high-fidelity ground truth for the library prior to downstream experimental phases.

\subsubsection{MORF transfection into HEK cells}\label{morf-transfection-into-hek-cells}

HEK cells were transfected with 2.5 μg of MORF plasmid library using TransIT-LT1 Transfection Reagent (Mirus Bio; MIR 2304) following the manufacturer's protocol. Briefly, 2.5 μg plasmid library was mixed into 250 μL OPTI-MEM and 7.5 μL TransIT-LT1 Reagent before incubating for 15 minutes at room temperature. Mixture was added to cells in a 6-well dish \textasciitilde80\% confluency drop-wise to different areas in the well. Sample was gently rocked to evenly distribute the mix within complete media. To verify successful transfection, a plasmid expressing GFP (Addgene \#145025; TFORF3549 GFP) was transfected in parallel in a separate well to check expression under a fluorescent microscope. Cells were harvested 72 hours post-transfection and total RNA was isolated using RNeasy Plus Mini Kit (Qiagen; cat \# 74134) (1474 ng/μL in 60 μL water).

\subsubsection{MORF transcript structure and RNA processing}\label{morf-transcript-structure-and-rna-processing}

Each MORF insert is a transcription factor open reading frame followed by a 24 bp isoform-specific barcode, carried in the lentiviral vector pLX\_TRC317 (derived from pLEX\_307) between the 5\p{} LTR and the WPRE \citep{joung2023}. Because the library was delivered as transfected plasmid rather than as packaged virus, transcription initiates from the plasmid at both the 5\p{} LTR and the internal EF-1$\alpha$ promoter, and reads are observed from both transcription start sites.

To establish which aspects of RNA processing this benchmark does and does not capture, we examined the MORF PacBio reads before 5\p{} soft-clip trimming, aligned to the full plasmid sequence. The transcripts are capped: reads beginning at either vector transcription start site carry the untemplated-G template-switching signature, with a G-run of at least two, in 89.9\% (5\p{} LTR) and 84.7\% (EF-1$\alpha$) of cases, against 6.0\% for reads beginning at the dominant internal truncation position, a separation comparable to that of the bulk PacBio cell line libraries. The vector carries the full EF-1$\alpha$ promoter including its first intron, which lies in the 5\p{} UTR and is spliced in 95.9\% of the reads that resolve it. The cloned open reading frames are themselves intronless cDNA, so no splicing occurs within the benchmarked isoform structures, and the structural differences among MORF isoforms are those already present in the clones. A polyadenylation signal is present in the 3\p{} LTR, downstream of the barcode and WPRE; its usage is not measurable in these libraries, which were primed from the WPRE rather than from the poly(A) tail. Because the benchmark annotation is restricted to the open reading frame and the transcript ends are vector-defined, MORFs were not used to evaluate transcription start or polyadenylation site inference.

\subsubsection{cDNA synthesis of MORF transcripts from HEK MORF total RNA}\label{cdna-synthesis-of-morf-transcripts-from-hek-morf-total-rna}

MORF transcripts (0.21 μL (300 ng) of HEK\_MORF total RNA at 1474 ng/μL) were primed for reverse transcription using a custom primer annealing to the WPRE located on the 3\p{} end of the MORF transcripts (DBO140 {[}N14 UMI{]}; IDT) and cDNA synthesis was performed using Pacbio Iso-Seq express 2.0 kit (PacBio; cat no. 103-071-50) following manufacturer\textquotesingle s protocol. cDNA was prepared for PacBio sequencing using the Kinnex full-length RNA kit (PacBio; cat. no 103-072-000). Sequencing was performed on the PacBio Revio system. The paired libraries before the concatenation were also sequenced on ONT MinION platform.

\subsubsection{MORF data processing}\label{morf-data-processing}

PacBio Kinnex reads were segmented using skera (v1.2.0) and demultiplexed using lima (v2.9.0). For ONT data, raw FASTQ reads were demultiplexed and trimmed after identifying 5\p{} and 3\p{} adapters based on Levenshtein distance (allowing for up to 1 mismatch) within a 150bp window. Following demultiplexing, reads from both platforms were mapped to the MORF insert reference (containing ORFs and isoform-specific barcodes) using minimap2 (v2.28) \citep{li2018}. We disabled secondary alignments to ensure mapping uniqueness and applied a strict end-anchoring filtration step, retaining only primary, forward-strand alignments that extended exactly to the final nucleotide of the isoform-specific barcodes to verify 3\p{}-end completeness. Reads were assigned to isoforms according to their corresponding barcoded reference sequence mappings.

Following initial alignment and filtering, soft-clipped bases were trimmed, and the final 14 nucleotides of each read were extracted as a UMI. To establish true molecular counts and remove PCR duplicates, deduplicated based on isoform assignments and UMI sequences by iterating through the alignments and retaining only the first primary alignment encountered for each unique isoform-UMI pair; all subsequent reads sharing the same signature were discarded.

Subsequently, this deduplicated dataset underwent a rigorous, systematic structural verification. To facilitate the analysis, a custom mini-genome of MORFs was constructed using the minigenome toolkit \citep{haas2017}. The initial genome assembly was generated by extracting genes for MORFs from the reference genome, shrinking introns and organizing MORF genes into single-contig representations, all derived from the GRCh38 reference assembly and Gencode v39 annotations \citep{gencodend}.We reconciled the deduplicated alignments with the MORF minigenome reference by extracting the intron chains and strand orientation for every retained read. A molecule was only included in the final benchmark if the read perfectly matched the annotated splice junction chain and strand orientation of its barcode-assigned isoform. For benchmarking, following the same strategy as our simulated mouse and Arabidopsis datasets, we derived two ground-truth reference sets from the MORF minigenome annotation: an Expressed Reference containing only isoforms with non-zero molecular counts, and a Reduced Reference with exactly one random isoform per gene.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Generation of Long-read Transcriptomic Data for FTD Frontal Cortex Single Nuclei }{Generation of Long-read Transcriptomic Data for FTD Frontal Cortex Single Nuclei }}\label{generation-of-long-read-transcriptomic-data-for-ftd-frontal-cortex-single-nuclei}

\textbf{Single nuclei isolation and cDNA library preparation from FTD postmortem frontal cortex:} Postmortem medial frontal cortex brain tissue from a female donor with FTLD-TDP due to a progranulin mutation was obtained from the Mayo Clinic. On ice, approximately 45mg of tissue was homogenized using a Dounce homogenizer with 20 strokes of the loose pestle followed by 20 strokes of the tight pestle in 500uL chilled 1x Lysis Buffer 1 (10mM Tris-HCl, 10mM NaCl, 3mM MgCl2, 0.1\% Nonidet P40 Substitute/IGEPAL CA-630 , 1mM DTT and 1U/ul RNase inhibitors (Sigma-Aldrich, 03335402001)). Homogenate was incubated 4 minutes on ice then neutralized with 500uL chilled Wash Buffer (10mM Tris-HCl, 10mM NaCl, 3mM MgCl2, 1\% Bovine Serum Albumin, 0.1\% Tween-20, 1mM DTT). Large debris and cell clumps were removed by passing the suspension through a 70um Flowmi Cell Strainer. After lysis, nuclei were collected by centrifugation through a sucrose cushion gradient. Two sucrose gradient tubes were prepared by adding 500uL 1.8M Sucrose Cushion Buffer 1 (Sigma-Aldrich, NUC201-1KT), with the nuclei suspension carefully layered on top. Samples were then centrifuged at 16049 x g for 45 minutes at 4ºC in a swinging bucket rotor. Myelin and debris were removed, and nuclei were resuspended in 500uL Wash Buffer. These were passed through a 40um Flowmi Cell Strainer, and aliquots were combined and centrifuged at 500 x g for 5 minutes at 4ºC in a fixed angle rotor. The pellet was resuspended in 500uL PBS Buffer with 1\% BSA and 1U/ul RNase inhibitors and counted using AO/PI on a Denovix CellDrop (Denovix, CD-AO-PI-1.5).

The resulting nuclei were processed using the 10x Genomics Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 3\p{} Reagents Kits v3.1 (10x Genomics, PN-1000268) and loaded on Chromium Next GEM Chip G (10x Genomics, 2000177) for targeted recovery of 10,000 nuclei. cDNA libraries were constructed as directed in the user guide (10x Genomics, CG000315 Rev. F). All libraries were quantified using Qubit dsDNA HS reagents (Invitrogen, Q33231). The average fragment size was determined using DNA HSD5000 screentape analysis (Agilent Technologies, 5067-5593, 5067-5592). cDNA libraries were between 400-2500 bp with an average size of 909bp. The resulting single nuclei cDNA libraries were then prepared for long read sequencing using Kinnex.

The library was sequenced across four PacBio flowcells and processed with the Kinnex single-cell workflow following PacBio\textquotesingle s recommended Iso-Seq processing guidelines. Raw HiFi reads from each flowcell were de-concatenated with skera to generate segmented reads (S-reads), with a consistently high proportion of full-length arrays across all flowcells (95.3--96.1\%). Primers were removed with lima, cell barcodes and UMIs were clipped from the reads and stored as BAM tags with isoseq tag, and poly(A) tails and residual artificial concatemers were removed with isoseq refine to yield full-length non-concatemer (FLNC) reads. FLNC reads from the four flowcells were merged, cell barcodes were corrected against the 10x barcode whitelist with isoseq correct, and PCR duplicates with shared cell barcodes and UMIs were deduplicated with isoseq groupdedup. The deduplicated reads were then aligned to the reference genome and used as input to LRAA, with corrected cell barcodes (CB) and UMIs (XM) persisting as BAM tags.

\subsection{Benchmarking Isoform Identification and Quantification}\label{benchmarking-isoform-identification-and-quantification}

Benchmarking methods and related code were adapted from that used for the Isosceles paper \citep{kabza2024} (\url{https://github.com/Genentech/Isosceles}). As such, benchmarking is restricted to spliced isoforms where isoforms are uniquely identified according to their patterns of spliced introns. If multiple isoforms have shared splice patterns but differ at termini boundaries (alternative TSS or TES), they are collapsed into single representative isoforms and associated expression values or read counts are aggregated.

Isoform identification and quantification tools and software versions benchmarked included the following: LRAA v0.51.0, Bambu v3.12.1, ESPRESSO v1.6.0, FLAMES v0.1, FLAIR v3.0.0, Isosceles v0.2.0, IsoQuant v3.13.0, IsoSeq v4.2.0, Mandalorion v4.5.0, Oarfish v0.9.4, StringTie v3.0.3, and TALON v6.0.

Isoform quantification accuracy was evaluated after converting transcript-level estimates from each method into a common splice-pattern representation. For each reference or predicted transcript, we derived an intron-chain identifier from the corresponding GTF annotation; for methods with quantification-only methods that report transcript IDs against the reference annotation, the reference GTF was used, whereas methods that produce their own transcript models (lacking a quantification-only method) were provided with the full reference GTF as a guide and results were adapted by mapping their method-specific GTF to reference truth isoforms based on identically matching splicing patterns. Transcript estimates sharing the same intron-chain identifier were collapsed by summing expression values.

For simulated, SIRV, and MORF benchmarks with defined expression truth, comparisons were anchored to the benchmark truth set. The truth table was constructed from the full benchmark reference annotation and expression file, retaining multi-exon reference isoforms from genes with nonzero truth expression, including zero-expression isoforms within those expressed genes. Each method's collapsed estimates were reindexed to this reference truth universe; reference isoforms not reported by a method were assigned estimated TPM of zero. Truth and estimated TPM values were then independently re-normalized to sum to 1M across the evaluated reference isoform set before computing accuracy metrics.

\textbf{Isoform quantification accuracy}: For each evaluated reference isoform, the absolute relative difference (ARD) between its truth expression $x$ and the method's estimate $y$ (both in TPM) was computed as

\[\text{ARD} = \frac{|x - y|}{\frac{x + y}{2}},\]

which ranges from 0 (exact agreement) to 2 (one of the two is zero). Isoforms with both truth and estimated TPM equal to zero were excluded. Each method's accuracy on a dataset is summarized as the mean relative difference (MRD), the mean ARD over the evaluated reference isoforms; for the SIRV mixes, which were spiked into four libraries (BT474, GM24385/HG002, K562 and UHRR), the per-library MRDs were averaged. Under this scoring scheme, missed expressed truth isoforms and expression assigned to zero-expression reference isoforms both contribute the maximal error. For methods lacking a strict quantification-only approach, any non-reference predicted isoforms were excluded from the quantification-only evaluation and ARD calculation.

\textbf{Isoform identification accuracy}: Predicted isoforms are assigned as true positives (TP) based on matching reference isoform structures, false positives (FP) if missing from the reference annotation, or false negatives (FN) if present in the reference annotation but not predicted. Summary accuracy statistics true positive rate (TPR or aka. recall), positive predictive value (PPV aka. precision), and F1 scores are computed as follows:
\[\text{TPR} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}, \qquad
\text{PPV} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}, \qquad
\text{F1} = \frac{2\,\text{TP}}{2\,\text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}}.\]

For annotation-free de novo isoform identification accuracy, reference annotations are not provided to prediction methods as input. All predicted isoforms are assigned TP, FP, or FN status based on comparison to the reference annotation truth set. For reference-guided isoform identification accuracy, predicted or reference isoforms corresponding to the splice patterns of those isoforms leveraged as guides were excluded from contributing to the benchmarking accuracy calculations. Reference-guided isoform benchmarking experiments limited the reference isoform guide to a single isoform per gene.

When an exact truth set is not available, the truth set is the GENCODE v39 isoforms with nonzero expression estimated by Oarfish (alignment mode); predictions matching unexpressed reference isoforms are not scored. Isoforms predicted by at least one other method and not found within the reference annotation are ignored so that they do not contribute as TP or penalized as FP.

The accuracy values plotted in \textbf{Figures~\ref{fig:2}--\ref{fig:3}} and \textbf{Supplementary Figures~\ref{sfig:2}--\ref{sfig:4}}, for each tool, benchmark and regime and for each sample they average over, are given in \textbf{Supplementary Table~3}.

The benchmarking framework implemented here is available on GitHub at \url{https://github.com/MethodsDev/LRAA_Isoform_ID_n_Quant_Bmark_Platform} with all evaluated methods' predicted isoform structures and quantifications available at Zenodo (\url{https://zenodo.org/records/20719930}).

\subsection{LRAA SQANTI-like read alignment and isoform structure annotation comparison}\label{lraa-sqanti-like-read-alignment-and-isoform-structure-annotation-comparison}

We incorporated a SQANTI \citep{pardopalacios2024} -like reference annotation comparison utility into the LRAA software suite to facilitate annotation of read alignments (bam) or isoform structures (GTF), considered `input features', in comparison to a reference annotation. This SQANTI-like annotation utility was designed to be a flexible light-weight and efficient system for assigning input features to annotation categories based on references annotation splice pattern matching and genomic feature localization. Annotation categories are assigned separately for spliced and unspliced input features (\textbf{Supplementary Figure~\ref{sfig:5}}).
